## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

Rain on polling day is widely believed to depress turnout and help the right, yet evidence comes mostly from the United States and a few European countries, and Britain lacks a long-run study. This paper matches 14,052 contested constituency contests at the 22 UK general elections from 1945 to 2024 to hourly ERA5 rain in each election's polling hours, testing hypotheses pre-specified in a written plan. With seat and election fixed effects, each centimetre of rain is associated with turnout 0.96 points lower (95% CI -1.82 to -0.11), near a Danish estimate. But inference that respects the few elections does not establish the effect (wild cluster bootstrap p = 0.14; map-swap randomisation inference p = 0.10), a day-before placebo is as large and at least as unusual, and there is no partisan effect. It bounds weather's role in British turnout and shows how uncertain rainfall designs are with few weather draws.
